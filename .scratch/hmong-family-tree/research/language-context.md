# Language context for Hmong family history

Retrieved: 2026-11-13. Bounded planning research, not a translation pack or a decision on launch scope. Family requirements below come from the assigned brief, not from linguistic sources.

## Findings

### Variety names need regional context

White Hmong is Hmoob Dawb, also called Hmong Daw; Hmong Der is used in [Lee's naming statement](https://www.garyyialee.com/topical-12-point-statement). Green Mong is also called Mong Leng or Mong Njua; Garellek and Esposito give Moob Lees and Moob Ntsuab. Their original phonetics study describes White Hmong spoken in Minnesota diaspora communities, not every regional variety. Its vowel comparisons demonstrate differences that affect spelling, so Green localization needs its own review rather than mechanical replacement of White forms. See section 1 of [Garellek and Esposito, Phonetics of White Hmong vowel and tonal contrasts](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-international-phonetic-association/article/phonetics-of-white-hmong-vowel-and-tonal-contrasts/42CAB2CD61855791611A1DFFD5BC407C).

The University of Minnesota corpus project's indexed introduction additionally uses Moob Leeg, Green Hmong/Mong and Blue Hmong/Mong. Its live page returned an application shell without readable introductory content, so these are discovery leads, not independently confirmed preferred labels: [Hmong Corpus](https://hmong-corpus.umn.edu/). Lee's own statement documents historical use of Green Hmong and Moob Ntsuab alongside Mong Leng, showing that naming preferences have a history: [12 Point Statement, On the name "Hmong"](https://www.garyyialee.com/topical-12-point-statement). Ask speakers which labels they use; do not assume all these names identify identical local speech.

Other relevant names include Hmoob Dub and Hmoob Shua. Kou Yang reports these from a conversation in Lao Cai, Vietnam, alongside Hmoob Shib, Leeg, Dawb and Ntsuab (printed p. 14). This is a field report of subgroup names, not a validated list of interchangeable language packs. It says "five" but lists six names; it also says the author did not meet Ntsuab speakers there. Preserve those limits. His framing separates Hmong from A Hmao, Hmu and Qho Xiong within the broader Miao designation (pp. 1, 4-5). Do not turn every Miao subgroup into a Hmong locale: [Yang, A Visit to the Hmong of Asia](https://www.hmongstudiesjournal.org/uploads/4/5/8/7/4587788/kouyang2008.pdf).

### Spoken variety, written variety and script are different choices

White/Green identify language varieties. RPA, the Romanized Popular Alphabet, is an orthography using Latin script. Pahawh Hmong and Nyiakeng Puachue Hmong are distinct scripts, not alternative names for White/Green. Unicode documents Pahawh's multiple stages and Nyiakeng Puachue's use for both White and Green. Encoding support establishes available characters, not reader literacy or a universally preferred spelling: [Unicode 17.0, sections 16.11-16.12](https://www.unicode.org/versions/Unicode17.0.0/core-spec/chapter-16/).

White's original diaspora study documents differences in phonology, grammar and vocabulary and contact between White, Green and English (pp. 157-162). Huster's participant study records the label "Hmonglish" in reported communication (p. 25). Neither defines a standardized app register. Treat the requested Hmonglish experience as a human editorial choice about how English and Hmong terms appear together: [White, Language and variety mixing in diasporic Hmong](https://www.italian-journal-linguistics.com/app/uploads/2021/07/8_White.pdf), [Huster, Generation 1.5 Hmong college women](https://www.hmongstudiesjournal.org/uploads/4/5/8/7/4587788/husterhsj13.pdf).

### Supplied terms and dependable sources

- **"Niam Tais"**: Bee Yang's community-authored curriculum explicitly lists this spelling as "Maternal grandmother", in Session IV's vocabulary and maternal-side list. Heimbach's White Hmong dictionary additionally gives maternal grandmother or the wife of a maternal grandfather's brother (printed p. 140, PDF page 95): [1979 dictionary scan](https://renincorp.org/bookshelf/white-hmong-english-diction.pdf#page=95). These attestations do not establish the user's intended meaning or variety. The section does not identify a White/Green scope: [Hmong Ways of Knowing, chapter 2](https://camodelcurricula.ucdavis.edu/hmong-ways-knowing-chapter-2).
- **"Pauj"**: The supplied spelling is preserved. Heimbach lists `pauj` as reciprocating an obligation, avenging, and a sentence-final particle, not a kinship noun (printed pp. 225-226, PDF page 138): [dictionary entries](https://renincorp.org/bookshelf/white-hmong-english-diction.pdf#page=138). No kinship meaning for the exact supplied form was verified. Lee attests the different spelling **"phauj"** for father's sister in his White Hmong discussion. Heimbach independently lists `phauj` as father's sisters (printed p. 241, PDF page 146): [entry](https://renincorp.org/bookshelf/white-hmong-english-diction.pdf#page=146). That is a comparison to ask about, not permission to correct "Pauj". Its intended pronunciation, spelling system, variety and relationship remain unknown: [Lee, White Hmong Kinship, Discussion](https://www.garyyialee.com/culture-white-hmong-kinship).
- **Source use**: Lee's 1986 analysis is useful for White Hmong relationship paths and differences between address and reference. Its web transcription has apparent errors and chart content not available as searchable prose. Pair it with community review. Bee Yang's curriculum offers teaching vocabulary but fewer relational distinctions. Neither alone validates a Green Mong term bank. Obtain a Green-specific dictionary or kinship analysis and fluent reviewer before claiming Green coverage. Lee's references point to Lemoine's Green Hmong work, but that original work was not verified here. Dictionary candidates and access limits are recorded below.

## Practical implications for this family

These are planning deductions from the brief, informed by the distinctions above, not a proposed API or a claim that all families use one system.

- Keep the woman who is cousin through dad and aunty through mom as one person with both relationship paths. A global translated label would hide the difference. Do not infer the Hmong terms from those English labels alone.
- Keep genealogical generation separate from birth order and age. Lee distinguishes generation, relative age, speaker and linking relative; his cousin categories also differ from English. A younger aunty remains possible. Require speaker, path and intended address/reference context before validating a term.
- Preserve multiple partnerships and distinguish biological, adoptive and care relationships. The brief requires all three. Lee describes multiple wives historically, but that account supplies no universal rule for contemporary partnerships, adoption or care terminology. Missing vocabulary must not erase a link or invent biological descent.
- Record a built-in term's variety, orthography, relationship conditions, source location and reviewer. Preserve family wording separately from a suggested term. These are evidence needs, not decisions about which controls or locales to ship.

## Unresolved human choices

1. Which regional/diaspora communities and preferred White/Green labels should reviewers represent? Which other varieties matter to the actual families?
2. Does "Hmonglish" mean English sentences retaining Hmong kinship terms, paired labels, or another register? Which written forms do those readers use?
3. For "Niam Tais" and "Pauj", who says the term to whom, along which path, and in which variety? Obtain pronunciation or an example sentence with consent; no private family data is needed for this report.
4. Who can validate each built-in term, including adoption/care usage and conflicting relationship paths? When should the family choose an address term?
5. If a label is called "official", official according to which named institution/community, document, region, variety and date? A dictionary entry, Unicode encoding or ISO language identifier does not establish universal cultural authority. ISO code mappings were not confirmed here and must not be used to collapse communities or decide mutual intelligibility.
