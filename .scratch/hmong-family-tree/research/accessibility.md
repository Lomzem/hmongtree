# Accessibility requirements for family exploration and printable selection

Research date and retrieval date for every external source below: **2026-11-13**. Status: research recommendations for parent integration; **no recommendation in this document is a user decision**. Assigned branch: `research/family-accessibility`.

## Scope and proposed target

The supplied brief establishes interactive exploration, explaining how two people are related, Hmong/English, women fully visible, private invited-family access, and a printable selection. The parent's uncommitted planning map was unavailable; this report does not infer its decisions. No application, UI, library, architecture, issue, tracker, or PR changes are proposed as settled implementation.

**Recommend WCAG 2.2 Level AA for the complete web experience**, including invitation/sign-in, exploration, relationship lookup, selection and printable HTML. AA includes all A and AA criteria; conformance applies to whole pages, responsive variants and complete processes. This report is a feature-focused checklist, not an exhaustive WCAG audit or a legal compliance determination. Prefer an accessible presentation within the same experience; a separate alternate version has formal conformance conditions and must not be assumed sufficient merely because it exists. [WCAG 2.2, §§5.1–5.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#conformance-reqs).

Additionally recommend two explicit product commitments beyond AA: disable nonessential interaction animation on request (SC 2.3.3, AAA), and aim for 44×44 CSS-pixel primary touch controls (SC 2.5.5, AAA, with exceptions). Neither amounts to a whole-site AAA claim. [Animation from interactions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html), [target-size guidance distinguishing 24 and 44](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html).

**Evidence labels:** N = normative WCAG requirement at the proposed A/AA target; G = informative WAI explanation, tutorial, APG or technique; R = researcher's proposed product acceptance rule/inference. Understanding pages reproduce the success criterion but their explanation is informative. The linked WCAG Recommendation is the normative authority. PDF/UA is a separate document standard; it is not automatically achieved by meeting web WCAG.

## Family graph and equivalent exploration

**N:** Information and relationships communicated visually must also be programmatically determinable or available in text (1.3.1 A). A meaningful reading sequence must be programmatically determinable where order affects meaning (1.3.2 A). [Info and relationships](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/info-and-relationships.html), [meaningful sequence](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/meaningful-sequence.html).

**G:** WAI's complex-image tutorial recommends a short identification plus a structured long description carrying essential graph information. That helps describe a static diagram; a description alone does not provide the functionality of an interactive explorer. [Complex images](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/complex/).

**N:** Functionality needs a keyboard interface without specific keystroke timing (2.1.1 A), and keyboard focus must be escapable (2.1.2 A). The path-dependent exception concerns the underlying function, not the choice to implement it with dragging. [Keyboard](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/keyboard.html), [no keyboard trap](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/no-keyboard-trap.html).

**R — observable acceptance:** Without seeing or dragging the graph, an invited user can find a person, distinguish same-name people using permitted identifying information, read that person's details, follow every supported relationship category, expand/load more relatives, return to the previous person, choose both people for a relationship query, and select people for printing. A structured person-and-relationships presentation can satisfy this outcome; WCAG does not prescribe a particular widget or mandate a separate list screen. Check that it exposes the same authorized information and operations as visual exploration, including relatives not currently drawn in the viewport.

**R — relationship result:** Show a named, ordered chain with explicit direction and relationship at each step, not only highlighted edges or a kinship label. A synthetic example is “Person A → parent Person B → sibling Person C → child Person D”; human reviewers must approve actual relationship terminology. State when results are incomplete because of selection/filter/data limits, and distinguish “no known connection in available records” from a claim of no relationship. Multiple-path policy and culturally correct terms remain human choices. These rules operationalize the information/relationship and reading-order requirements; WCAG does not specify genealogy reasoning.

**N/G:** WAI-ARIA defines a `tree` as a hierarchically organized selection widget; APG's tree-view pattern describes a hierarchical list with expansion. A family network with partnerships, multiple parentage or repeated ancestors is not automatically that hierarchy. **R:** Do not assign tree semantics merely because the product calls it a family tree. If a genuine hierarchical projection is chosen, test its APG keyboard contract: arrows for traversal/expansion, Home/End, Enter, and clear separation of focus and selection; type-ahead is recommended by APG. These are pattern recommendations, not a WCAG requirement to use a tree. [WAI-ARIA tree definition](https://www.w3.org/TR/wai-aria-1.2/#tree), [APG tree view](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/treeview/).

**R — women's visibility and privacy:** Using a synthetic family with women in multiple generations, confirm equal discoverability, readable names, person navigation, relationship paths and print eligibility for women and men. Avoid making a woman accessible only through a male relative's card. This is an explicit brief requirement, not a gender-specific WCAG criterion. Apply identical authorization filtering to text, accessible names/descriptions, status messages, visual content and exports: an accessibility alternative must not reveal otherwise hidden people. Use synthetic families in evidence and never upload private family records to public audit services.

## Pan, zoom, dragging and touch

**N:** Multipoint/path-based author gestures require an equivalent single-pointer operation without a path-based gesture unless essential (2.5.1 A). Dragging functionality needs an equivalent single-pointer action without dragging unless essential or unmodified user-agent behavior (2.5.7 AA). These are independent of keyboard access. Native browser scrolling differs from author-controlled canvas panning. [Pointer gestures](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pointer-gestures.html), [dragging movements](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html).

**R — observable acceptance:** With touch using individual taps, reach offscreen graph regions and people, zoom in/out and reset/recenter. Repeat with keyboard alone. Explicit pan-direction and zoom controls are one candidate, not an implementation decision. If lasso, node movement, selection dragging or viewport minimap dragging is introduced, specify and verify an equivalent click/tap workflow for each operation. Reset alone is insufficient if it cannot achieve the operation. Pinch, wheel and drag may remain conveniences. Preserve browser zoom and ordinary page navigation; do not require a hardware keyboard for the tap alternative.

**N:** Pointer targets are at least 24×24 CSS pixels or meet a documented 2.5.8 AA exception. The spacing exception tests a 24-pixel-diameter circle centered on each undersized target's bounding box against other targets/circles; it is not a universal 24-pixel gap rule. Other exceptions cover equivalent controls on the same page, inline targets, unmodified user-agent sizing and essential presentation. [Target size minimum](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html).

**R — observable acceptance:** Measure interactive hit areas and spacing at supported graph scales, including zoomed-out nodes, disclosure buttons, close controls and print checkboxes. Prefer 44×44 primary controls; avoid invoking essential presentation merely because dense graph nodes are convenient. If a same-page equivalent is relied upon, demonstrate that it performs the same operation. Pointer cancellation must also satisfy 2.5.2 A; a suitable simple policy is activation on release with an opportunity to abort. [Pointer cancellation](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pointer-cancellation.html).

## Focus, reading order and changing content

**N:** Sequential focus preserves meaning/operation (2.4.3 A); focus is visible (2.4.7 AA) and not entirely obscured by author-created content (2.4.11 AA). Reading and focus order need not be identical, but both must make sense. [Focus order](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-order.html), [focus visible](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-visible.html), [focus not obscured](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html).

**R — observable acceptance:** Traverse from search to results, selected person, relationship query and print selection at narrow/wide widths. Opening details has a predictable focus destination; dismissing an overlay returns to its opener or a sensible surviving control. Expanding, filtering, loading and layout changes do not silently strand focus in removed content or move it to the document start. A focused graph person is brought into view. Prefer fully visible focus, beyond AA's “not entirely hidden” floor. Provide a way past a large graph to the surrounding content; do not force traversal of thousands of nodes to reach Print.

**N:** Controls expose accessible names, roles, states and values (4.1.2 A); visible labels occur within accessible names (2.5.3 A). Qualifying status messages are exposed without requiring focus (4.1.3 AA). [Name, role, value](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/name-role-value.html), [label in name](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/label-in-name.html), [status messages](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/status-messages.html).

**R — observable acceptance:** Screen readers announce a person's identity, the purpose of actions, expansion and print-selection states. Search/loading/result-count/no-result/export-ready messages are perceivable without unexpected focus changes. Do not announce every graph coordinate update; verify useful summaries rather than repetitive noise. Details do not depend on hover alone. If hover/focus popups are used, test dismissibility, pointer movement into the popup and persistence, subject to SC 1.4.13's exceptions. [Hover/focus content](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/content-on-hover-or-focus.html).

## Responsive layout, enlargement and graph exceptions

**N:** 1.4.10 AA requires ordinary vertically scrolling content to work at 320 CSS-pixel width without loss or two-dimensional scrolling; the corresponding horizontal-content height is 256 CSS pixels. There is an exception for parts requiring two-dimensional layout for usage or meaning. WAI includes diagrams among examples, while explaining that the surrounding content and content within such layouts still need consideration. [Reflow and interpretation](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html).

**R:** A spatial family diagram may justify a local two-dimensional viewport; document why spatial meaning requires it. This is not a blanket family-app exemption. Search, person descriptions, relationship explanations, invitations, selection controls and print setup should reflow. Avoid applying the exception to all text cards by association. Provide the structured equivalent described above and test it independently.

**N:** Text supports enlargement to 200% without lost content/functionality, except captions/images of text (1.4.4 AA). Text-spacing overrides must not lose content/functionality where supported by the language/script (1.4.12 AA). [Resize text](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/resize-text.html), [text spacing](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/text-spacing.html).

**R — observable acceptance:** Test 320 CSS-pixel width and a 1280-wide starting viewport at 400% browser zoom; separately test 200% enlargement. Preserve full names, relationships and controls; graph zoom is not a substitute for browser/text enlargement. Apply simultaneous spacing overrides: line height 1.5× font size, paragraph spacing 2×, letter spacing 0.12×, word spacing 0.16×. These are override-resilience tests, not mandatory default styling. Document every claimed exception rather than quietly excluding the graph from testing. Test portrait and landscape; do not require rotation for ordinary exploration.

## Relationship encoding and motion

**N:** Color cannot be the only visual encoding (1.4.1 A). Text contrast is normally at least 4.5:1, with 3:1 for large text and the criterion's exceptions (1.4.3 AA). Required graphic parts and control/state identification need 3:1 against adjacent colors, with applicable exceptions (1.4.11 AA). [Color](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html), [text contrast](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html), [non-text contrast](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html).

**R — observable acceptance:** In grayscale and with print backgrounds disabled, identify selected people, the relationship path and each supported edge meaning using labels and/or clearly distinguishable line/shape encoding with a legend. Screen-reader text explicitly states edge meanings. Color may supplement these. Check names and edges on actual backgrounds, selected states and focus states; do not treat women as lower-contrast secondary content.

**N:** Auto-starting moving/blinking/scrolling information lasting over five seconds alongside other content needs pause/stop/hide unless essential; automatic information updates have a separate rule without that five-second condition (2.2.2 A). Flashing must satisfy 2.3.1 A. [Pause, stop, hide](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html), [flash threshold](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html).

**R:** Prefer no flashing. Disable nonessential animated zoom flights, panning transitions and graph rearrangements when reduced motion is requested, preserving the resulting information and position. This adds the proposed AAA interaction-animation commitment; it is not an assertion that every animation fails AA. Verify both motion-preference settings and any in-app motion control. [Animation guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html).

## Hmong/English and human terminology

**N:** Default page language is programmatically determinable (3.1.1 A), as are differently worded language passages/phrases (3.1.2 AA), with exceptions for proper names, technical terms, indeterminate language and adopted vernacular. [Language of page](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/language-of-page.html), [language of parts](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/language-of-parts.html).

**G:** W3C recommends BCP 47 tags chosen from IANA's registry. The registry identifies `en` as English, `hmn` as Hmong/Mong with macrolanguage scope, `mww` as Hmong Daw, and `hnj` as Hmong Njua/Mong Leng/Mong Njua, the latter two within `hmn`. These are registry facts, not a decision about this family's variety. [Choosing tags](https://www.w3.org/International/questions/qa-choosing-language-tags), [IANA registry, relevant language records](https://www.iana.org/assignments/language-subtag-registry/language-subtag-registry).

**R — observable acceptance:** Once humans choose translation variety/script, verify the default language changes with UI language and different-language passages carry truthful tags, including printable content. Keep names intact; do not translate proper names by assumption. Have fluent family reviewers assess kinship terms, gendered references, direction of relationship and parallel English wording. Provide a plain-language chain when a compact translated term is uncertain. WCAG does not require bilingual translation or select a Hmong dialect; those are product commitments.

**Limit:** Language tags cannot install a Hmong voice. Support and pronunciation require tests with actual target assistive technology and available voices. WAI also documents that flattened accessible names can lose embedded language changes; verify markup as well as spoken output and avoid promising perfect mixed-language speech. [Language-of-parts support limitation](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/language-of-parts.html).

## Printable selection and digital PDF

**G:** CSS supports media-specific presentation, including print. That is a layout mechanism, not a guarantee that a saved PDF retains document semantics. Do not use the obsolete CSS Print Profile as a current conformance baseline. [CSS media types](https://www.w3.org/TR/CSS2/media.html), [CSS Print Profile status](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-print/).

**R — paper acceptance:** Before invoking browser print, users can review the chosen people, selection scope and exclusions through keyboard and screen reader. The printable document includes readable names, relationship labels/legend, selection context and a structured relationship listing. It contains the selected authorized information, not merely the visible graph viewport; no clipped scroll regions, collapsed omissions or loading placeholders. Test long names, bilingual text, multi-page selections, grayscale and backgrounds off at agreed paper sizes. Large diagrams can use bounded sections with continuation references instead of shrinking all names until unreadable. A minimum print font size and supported paper sizes require human approval; WCAG specifies no universal print-point minimum.

**R — privacy acceptance:** Print only deliberately selected authorized content. Preview makes scope clear; private attributes are not silently added to accessible descriptions or metadata. Physical printouts and locally saved files outlive sign-in access; decide what export warning and allowed fields family members need. No export permission policy is inferred here.

**G:** WAI PDF techniques cover logical tag/reading/tab order (PDF3), image alternatives (PDF1), default language (PDF16), passage languages (PDF19) and title (PDF18). These techniques are informative examples of satisfying WCAG criteria, not a complete PDF accessibility certification checklist. [PDF3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/pdf/PDF3), [PDF1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/pdf/PDF1), [PDF16](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/pdf/PDF16), [PDF19](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/pdf/PDF19), [PDF18](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/pdf/PDF18).

**Standards boundary:** ISO 14289-2:2024 (PDF/UA-2) governs accessible PDF using PDF 2.0; ISO's abstract explicitly excludes conversion processes and presentation implementation details. Full ISO normative text was not reviewed or purchased, so this report cannot supply an exhaustive PDF/UA checklist. [ISO original abstract](https://www.iso.org/standard/82278.html).

**R — digital export acceptance, if promised:** Inspect the actual file from each supported browser/OS/export destination. Verify a meaningful title, language metadata, semantic tags, logical reading order, headings/lists, relationship alternatives and recoverable Unicode text; test Hmong names and passages for display, search, copy and assistive reading. Selectable text alone does not establish accessible structure. A tagged file alone does not prove correct tags, reading order or PDF/UA conformance. Follow automated document checks with keyboard and screen-reader reading in the supported PDF reader. Record exact browser, OS, destination and reader versions.

**Limit/open choice:** No browser-generated PDF, physical printout or current browser export implementation was tested in this assignment. Do not claim that all browser Save-as-PDF paths are accessible, or that all discard tags. An original Chromium announcement could not be retrieved reliably, so historical support snippets are not used as decisive evidence. Recommend accessible printable HTML as a verified baseline; a promise of accessible downloadable PDF needs its own accepted standard, supported export path and artifact-based verification. The requested “printable selection” does not itself settle that promise.

## Verification proposed for implementation

These are future acceptance procedures, not tests already passed by an app:

1. Use synthetic fixtures with several generations, women/men equally represented, repeated names, long bilingual names, repeated ancestors, multiple supported relationship categories, disconnected records and a restricted person. Compare visible facts/operations to the structured equivalent and every export.
2. Execute the full invited-user journey with keyboard only; with single taps and no dragging/pinch; and with screen reader reading/navigation. Include relationship lookup, empty/error/loading states, print selection and return from print. For invitation authentication, allow assistance such as paste/autofill for codes and audit the complete process under 3.3.8 AA. [Accessible authentication](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/accessible-authentication-minimum.html).
3. Repeat responsive, enlargement, text-spacing, contrast, target-size, focus and reduced-motion checks above. Capture exceptions and exact evidence, not only a passing automated scan.
4. Proposed initial manual matrix for human approval: desktop keyboard plus NVDA/Firefox and VoiceOver/Safari; mobile VoiceOver/Safari and TalkBack/Chrome; supported paper/PDF paths. This is a proposed coverage set, not a standards-prescribed matrix or a claim of testing. Include fluent Hmong reviewers and people who use assistive technology where feasible.
5. Verify paper layout and actual saved PDF separately. Keep synthetic evidence of pass/fail, versions, export settings, tested scope and remaining defects. Never infer accessibility from the HTML source, a screenshot or a library's claims alone.

## Open human choices and handoff

- Approve the proposed WCAG 2.2 AA target, motion addition and primary-touch sizing goal; agree claim scope and evaluator/browser/AT coverage.
- Choose Hmong variety/script, translators and family reviewers; resolve kinship terminology, multiple-path explanations and fallback wording without treating registry labels as cultural decisions.
- Decide initial graph scope and progressive exploration behavior, supported relationship categories, treatment of uncertain records and printing scope. Ensure women remain equally available under any chosen scope.
- Decide paper sizes, large-selection pagination and whether accessible digital PDF is a launch requirement or only printable HTML; if PDF is promised, choose the PDF standard and supported generation/reading paths before claiming conformance.
- Decide permitted export fields, how restrictions affect relationship explanations, and what users should be told about copies outside the private app.

Principles applied: **Experience First** led to task-equivalent person/relationship navigation rather than judging only graph controls. **Prove It Works** led to checking original standards and requiring evidence from real exported files, while marking all app/AT/PDF behavior untested. **Laziness Protocol** kept this assignment to one research artifact and no implementation or tracker changes.
